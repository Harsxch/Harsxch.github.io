DNA is a twisted ladder of four letters

A nucleotide = a pentose sugar (deoxyribose) + a phosphate + a nitrogen base. Nucleotides join by 3′–5′ phosphodiester bonds to make one strand. Two strands run in opposite directions (antiparallel) and are held together by hydrogen bonds between bases.

How did we learn DNA is the genetic material?

Two classic experiments settled it. Run both yourself.

Griffith, 1928: the "transforming principle"

S strain (smooth, capsule, virulent) and R strain (rough, no capsule, harmless) of Streptococcus pneumoniae. Choose what to inject into a mouse.

Hershey and Chase, 1952: labelled viruses

Bacteriophages infect E. coli. Label either the protein or the DNA with a radioactive tag, let them infect, spin in a blender and centrifuge. Where does the radioactivity end up?

Timeline

RememberAvery, MacLeod and McCarty (1944) purified the transforming principle and showed it was DNA, because only DNase destroyed it. Hershey and Chase used ³²P for DNA and ³⁵S for protein.

Copying DNA: semi-conservative replication

Each new DNA molecule keeps one old strand and builds one new strand. Replication starts at the origin (ori) and the helix opens into a replication fork.

Meselson and Stahl, 1958

E. coli grown for many generations in heavy ¹⁵N medium, then moved to light ¹⁴N medium. DNA is spun in a CsCl density gradient. Pick a generation.

NCERT numbersE. coli copies its 4.6 × 10⁶ bp in about 38 minutes, roughly 2000 bp per second. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only in the 5′→3′ direction, which is why one strand is made in pieces.

Transcription: DNA to RNA

RNA polymerase reads the template strand (3′→5′) and builds RNA 5′→3′. The RNA matches the coding strand, with U in place of T.

Exam tipBacteria use one RNA polymerase. Eukaryotes use three: Pol I (rRNAs), Pol II (mRNA precursor, hnRNA) and Pol III (tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNAs). A transcription unit has a promoter, the structural gene and a terminator.

The genetic code and translation

Three bases make one codon. 64 codons: 61 code for amino acids, AUG is the start (Met), and UAA, UAG, UGA are stop signals.

Properties of the codeTriplet · degenerate (many codons per amino acid) · unambiguous · non-overlapping · comma-less · nearly universal. Nirenberg, Khorana and Ochoa cracked it.
